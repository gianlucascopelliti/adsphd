\section{Dissertation Outline}
\label{thesis:intro-outline}

The remainder of this dissertation is structured as follows. \cref{ch:authexec}
focuses on process-based \acp{TEE}, presenting our framework to develop
distributed enclave applications on shared and untrusted infrastructures.
\cref{ch:cloud} then moves to \acs{VM}-based \acp{TEE}, discussing the
challenges to provision and attest \acp{CVM} in the cloud. Next, \cref{ch:time}
highlights the problem of getting realiable and consistent timing information
inside a \ac{TEE}. This knowledge is then used in \cref{ch:v2x} to design a
novel revocation scheme for \ac{V2X} credentials that leverages a trusted
component inside vehicles to guarantee timely revocation without relying on a
physical time source. Finally, \cref{ch:conclusion} summarizes this dissertation
and introduces possible directions for future work.

Chapters \ref{ch:authexec} to \ref{ch:v2x} are based on the corresponding
peer-reviewed publications, with minor layout adaptions. In addition, a preamble
is added at the beginning of each chapter to give some context based on the
subject of this dissertation.
